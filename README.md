## About

I'm a Threat Hunter at **TCS**, focused on investigating suspicious activity and translating threat intelligence into practical hunting hypotheses and detection use cases.

My work spans endpoint and identity-focused hunting, campaign analysis, and security telemetry across Microsoft and CrowdStrike environments. I examine process activity, account behaviour, file events, and network connections to understand what the evidence supports and where further validation is needed.

Alongside operational hunting, I explore adversary techniques in controlled labs and write about vulnerability research, attacker tradecraft, and defensive investigation methods.

My approach is straightforward: start with a hypothesis, follow the telemetry, consider benign explanations, and distinguish observed behaviour from confirmed malicious activity.

## Selected Work

### TEMP.Hex — Windows Threat Hunting Lab

A controlled Windows lab for exploring campaign-informed attacker behaviours and developing telemetry-based hunting ideas.

Scenarios include simulated USB execution, discovery and staging activity, beaconing, and Registry Run Key persistence. The focus is on understanding the execution flow, identifying observable signals, and documenting how to investigate them.

**Windows · Endpoint telemetry · Threat hunting · Controlled simulations**

### PHP2Shell — Vulnerability Research

A research write-up examining reporting around **CVE-2026-109618** and **CVE-2026-109619**, with a focus on reported vulnerability mechanics, potential impact, and defensive investigation opportunities.

The aim is to connect technical reporting with useful hunting hypotheses while distinguishing documented behaviour from assumptions that require further validation.

**Vulnerability research · Threat intelligence · Defensive analysis**

## How I Approach a Hunt

- Define the adversary behaviour and a clear hunting hypothesis.
- Identify the telemetry required to observe it.
- Develop queries around meaningful behavioural signals.
- Correlate activity across processes, accounts, devices, and time.
- Consider legitimate activity and visibility gaps.
- Document the evidence, limitations, and recommended validation.

**A query match is a starting point for investigation, not proof of compromise.**
